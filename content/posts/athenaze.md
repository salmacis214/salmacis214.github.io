It's been more than three weeks since my intro post so here's a mini life update: I'm learning Ancient Greek. Precisely, I got a copy of [*Athenaze Book I*](https://global.oup.com/ukhe/product/athenaze-book-i-9780190607661?cc=gb&lang=en&) (the UK edition) a couple of months back but I've only started working through it right now.

Greek is a pretty scary language. While its five-case noun declension system isn't that bad, certainly not as extensive as [Finno-Ugric languages](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/koira#Declension), its [verbal conjugation system](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CF%86%CE%AD%CF%81%CF%89#Conjugation) is massive to make up for it: conjugation for person (first, second, and third) and number (singular, plural, AND dual), as well as three voices -- active, passive, and this weird thing called the middle, which seems to make transitive verbs reflexive -- and the tenses and moods you probably recognise from Latin (except for the aorist, but I don't even know what that is). There's also a whole bunch of rules about accenting and enclitics and proclitics that you're advised to remember for composition.

That's not to say that *Athenaze* whacks you around the head with all this stuff from chapter 1 -- so far, it has introduced new grammatical material at a fairly sedate rate. It does introduce all the noun cases in the singular for certain nouns in Chapter 2, which was actually quite refreshing given my secondary-school Latin experiences of being drip-fed all the cases over the course of a couple years.
